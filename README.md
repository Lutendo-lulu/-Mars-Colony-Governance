# Governance & Resource Planning: Mars Colony Simulation
**EKN 310 — Public Economics and Fiscal Policy | University of Pretoria | 2026**

## Overview
This project applies public economics theory to a hypothetical but analytically rigorous scenario: designing a complete governance, resource allocation, and economic system for a 1,000-person Mars colony with no external supply chain, no contact with Earth, and an absolute survival window of two years before a self-sustaining system must be operational.

The project required integrating fiscal policy, institutional economics, monetary theory, and infrastructure planning into a coherent, evidence-based framework.

## The Problem
- 1,000 settlers aged 14–40, unknown demographic composition
- 6-month buffer of Earth-provided resources
- 2-year absolute survival limit without self-sufficiency
- No external resupply or technology transfer
- No pre-existing governance structure

## Framework Applied

### 1. Resource Allocation
Each settler allocated 75kg personal weight allowance; 50% contributed to a communal pool. Key allocations:
- 500kg nuclear energy system (power)
- 200g heirloom seeds per person (food security)
- 800g digital offline library device per person (education and knowledge)
- 3kg personal and common medications per person
- 2,000kg communal bioreactors and medical equipment
- 4,000kg 3D printers + 3,000kg electrolysis units (infrastructure and water/oxygen production)

### 2. Governance Structure
Proposed a **democratic constitutional system** grounded in public economics principles:
- Written constitution with defined separation of powers and succession rules
- Short electoral cycles with term limits to ensure accountability
- Judicial independence to enforce constitutional compliance
- Transparency requirements for resource allocation and policy decisions
- Recall procedures allowing removal of underperforming officials
- Emergency regulation time limits to prevent power concentration

### 3. Economic System
Phased monetary and fiscal model:
| Phase | System | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Early settlement | Barter system | Low transaction volume; no price mechanism needed |
| Growth phase | Digital fiat currency — Mars Credit (MC) | Increasing specialisation makes barter inefficient |
| Mature economy | Taxation + public spending | Fund infrastructure maintenance and public goods |

Monetary precedents drawn on: Nixon's removal of the gold standard (1971) and the Bahamas' central bank digital currency.

### 4. Public Infrastructure
Five critical infrastructure systems, all publicly funded:
- **In-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU):** Extracts water from ice, oxygen from atmosphere, and building materials from Martian rock
- **Modular nuclear power grids:** Stable, continuous electricity supply
- **Automated pressurised transport:** Moves settlers and resources between sites
- **Integrated agricultural hubs:** Food production and crop management
- **Mars-Net (communication and navigation):** Data sharing and precision navigation

## Key Argument
Colonising Mars is not just a survival challenge — it is a public economics problem. The sustainable allocation of scarce resources, the design of accountable institutions, and the creation of a functioning monetary system are the foundations that determine whether a colony survives or collapses. Democratic governance with transparent fiscal management is not idealistic — it is the most structurally stable option under conditions of resource scarcity and social diversity.
